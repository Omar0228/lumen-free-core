BOOK OF NETWORKING · CHAPTER 1

The Divided Kingdom — What Is a VLAN?

A VLAN, or Virtual Local Area Network, divides a switched network into separate logical Layer 2 broadcast domains.

Devices in different VLANs generally need a router or Layer 3 switch to communicate. Routing must be configured, and security policies must permit the traffic.

Gate 2 — Choose Your Learning Key

BLUE — Explain Like I'm Five

Imagine a school with two groups of classrooms. Even though everyone uses the same hallway, the school can separate which groups receive each other's announcements. VLANs help a network make groups like that.

Gate 3 — See the Network

Example: four computers connected to one Layer 2 switch.

PC1 VLAN 10
192.168.10.10
PC2 VLAN 10
192.168.10.20
PC3 VLAN 20
192.168.20.10
PC4 VLAN 20
192.168.20.20

Shared switch: All four PCs connect to one physical switch, but VLAN 10 and VLAN 20 are separate broadcast domains.

Without inter-VLAN routing: PC1 and PC2 can communicate within VLAN 10 when configured correctly. PC3 and PC4 can communicate within VLAN 20. PC1 cannot directly reach PC3 through this Layer 2 switch alone.

Diagram is conceptual. Actual communication also depends on cabling, interface status, addressing, host firewalls, and configuration. VLANs alone are not a complete security solution.
